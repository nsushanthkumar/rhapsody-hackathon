5-dimension framework. The key design principle is that no single dimension dominates — a team with zero working code but a brilliant, well-argued, high-impact idea can still beat a team that shipped something shallow. (see init_evaluation_framework.html)
The five dimensions in plain English:

Problem & Originality — Is it truly 0→1?
Business Impact — What happens if this gets built for real?
Solution & Implementation — How well designed and executed is it?
Persuasion & Pitch — Did they convince the room?
Cross-functional Collaboration — Did all roles actually contribute?

Summary 

The INIT(0→1) title is perfect. It's technical enough to excite engineers, conceptual enough to excite business folks, and it communicates the entire philosophy in one glance. Don't change it.
The cross-functional team structure is the smartest decision you've made. Most hackathons are tech-only echo chambers. Forcing a marketer, a manager, and a data scientist to solve one problem together — that's where genuinely different ideas come from. That's real collaboration, not just co-working.
The 0→1 filter as the north star is what separates this from a typical fun event. It gives the whole thing intellectual honesty. You're not just keeping people busy for 24 hours — you're asking them to create something that doesn't exist. That's meaningful.
The evaluation framework is fair and multi-dimensional. A brilliant idea can beat a shallow prototype. That's the right call.
The side quests are the secret weapon. They solve the biggest problem with overnight hackathons — the 2 AM energy crash. Push-ups, MonkeyType, helping other teams — that keeps the room alive and human.
