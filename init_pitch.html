The Challenge — Solving Real Pain Points

Many hackathons are just tech-focused echo chambers. They optimize for what is easy to demo and exclude the voices of those who know what clients actually want. INVENT' 26 is structured differently. The problem statements you will choose from are sourced directly from our Sales team, representing real, unmet market pain points. You will work in strictly cross-functional teams because each member brings a piece of the puzzle that the others miss.

This 18-hour continuous sprint is designed to turn internal talent into a product-creation engine for Global Capability Centers (GCCs). By pairing 5 Strategic Prompts with 4 High-Value GCC Domains, we form a Hackathon Matrix — guaranteeing maximum output variance and delivering a ready-to-demo portfolio.

How You're Scored

The Evaluation Framework

Three dimensions. None dominant.

The single most important design principle in INVENT'26: no single dimension wins on its own. A team with zero working code but a brilliant, well-argued, high-impact idea can still beat a team that shipped something shallow. A team with the slickest demo can still lose to a team that genuinely thought harder about the problem.

💡 Pro Tip — Free Points
Add a "Guided Tour" to your demo

One useful feature we're seeing in recent demos is a Guided Tour. From here, it's a small step to add audio. Claude Code, Codex, and other coding agents can add this easily with a one-line prompt — something like:

"Add a guided tour to this application, with timed transitions, visible simulated mouse clicks, and keyboard typing as required, to showcase key features. Explain using SPECIFIC insights at each step, showcasing why this helps."

In short: ask your teams to add a Guided Tour to their demos. Low effort, high impact — and it directly boosts your Persuasion & Pitch Quality score.

Handing It In

The Submission

Ship it. Prove it. Send us the link. That's the entire spec.

1
Drop a working link into the Google Form
Submit one link to the official Google Form before the 18-hour timer hits zero. When the judges click it, it must open, load, and work — no local demos, no "it works on my machine", no zip files.
2
Use whatever AI tools or deployment services you need
Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Windsurf, Bolt, v0, Lovable, Replit, Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, Hugging Face Spaces, Streamlit Cloud — use all of them, use none of them, we don't care. The only thing being judged is what shows up when the link loads.
A gentle nudge on what counts as "a link" A workable link is anything a judge can click without begging IT for access. A Claude Artifact, a Gemini Gem, an AI Studio share URL, a scrappy Vercel deploy, a Streamlit app duct-taped together at 3 AM, a Hugging Face Space, or literally a <canvas> you drew in a browser tab — all fair game. If it loads and does the thing, it counts. If it needs a Zoom call and your laptop to demo, it doesn't.
